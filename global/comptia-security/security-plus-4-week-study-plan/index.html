Security+ 4-Week Study Plan: Daily Schedule (701 and 801)

By Prostutii Editorial • Last Verified: 2026-11-18
⚡Direct Answer / Summary
This 4-week Security+ study plan assumes 8 to 10 hours a week, about 36 hours in total. Spend weeks 1 to 3 learning the five domains in proportion to their exam weights, then use week 4 for timed practice, weak-spot review and exam-day preparation. It works for SY0-701 and SY0-801, with hour adjustments for each.

Last verified: November 18, 2026, against CompTIA’s Security+ V7 and Security+ V8 pages. This plan is independent guidance, not an official CompTIA schedule.

A good study plan does three things: it covers every domain, it spends time in proportion to how much each domain is worth on the exam, and it builds in checkpoints so you find gaps early. This plan gives you a day-by-day schedule for four weeks, a lighter eight-week version, and an exam-day checklist. If you have not chosen an exam version yet, start with our SY0-701 vs SY0-801 guide or the decision calculator.

Key takeaways
✦ The plan uses about 36 hours: 27 hours of learning across three weeks, then 9 hours of review and practice.
✦ Time per domain follows the official weights, so Security Operations gets the most and the smallest domain gets the least.
✦ Each week ends with a checkpoint. Log your misses by topic and fix them before moving on.
✦ Week 4 introduces no new topics. It is for timed practice, weak spots and logistics.
✦ Complete beginners should use the eight-week version, or build networking basics first.

Is a 4-week plan right for you?

This plan assumes you can study 8 to 10 hours a week, which works out to about 36 hours over four weeks. For perspective, CompTIA’s V8 page estimates 25 to 40 hours for its CertMaster Learn course and 10 to 20 hours for CertMaster Practice. At roughly 36 hours, this plan sits at the low end of that range for learning plus practice, so it suits people who already have some IT or networking background. CompTIA lists a security administrator with two years of hands-on experience as the recommended profile for SY0-801.

Who should NOT use a 4-week plan.
If you are a complete beginner with no IT background, or you cannot study at least 8 hours a week, four weeks is likely too fast. Use the eight-week version below, add time for networking basics such as IP addressing, DNS and common ports, and rely on your practice scores, not the calendar, to decide when to book your exam.

Adjusting the schedule for your exam version

The daily schedule below follows the SY0-801 split. If you are taking SY0-701, make these small shifts:

  • Spend about 1.5 fewer hours on Domain 1 in week 1, since it carries 12% instead of 16%.
  • Replace the 30-minute AI risk block in week 2 with mitigation techniques, which sit in the threats domain on SY0-701.
  • Add about 1.5 hours of governance and risk in week 3, since Domain 5 carries 20% on SY0-701.

If you are taking SY0-801, add time on the new AI topics using our SY0-801 AI security scenarios. If you already studied for SY0-701, see the upgrade guide for a short bridge instead of restarting.

How to run a checkpoint.
1. Time yourself, about one minute per question.
2. Read every explanation, including for questions you got right.
3. Log each miss by topic, not by question number.
4. If you score under 15 out of 20, spend your next session on the weak topics before moving on.

Use our practice sets for the first two checkpoints: General Security Concepts and Threats and vulnerabilities. For vocabulary, use the acronyms and ports cheat sheet. For performance-based questions, see the PBQ guide.

Exam-day checklist

The real exam has a maximum of 90 questions in 90 minutes, a mix of multiple-choice and performance-based questions, and a passing score of 750 on a scale of 100 to 900. Use this list on the final weekend and the morning of your exam.

  1. Confirm the details. Check your appointment time, location or online-session instructions, and the ID your provider requires. Names must match your registration.
  2. Do logistics early. Plan your route and arrival time, or, if you test remotely, run the provider’s system and room checks in advance.
  3. Stop new material. The day before, do a light flashcard pass and sleep well. New topics at night rarely stick.
  4. Eat and hydrate. A steady morning beats a cramped last-minute review.
  5. Pace yourself. Aim for about one minute per question. Flag hard questions, move on, and return if time remains.
  6. Read for the deciding detail. Look for words such as BEST, FIRST and MOST, and eliminate options that are true but do not answer the question.
  7. Use your review time. If you finish early, revisit flagged questions rather than changing answers at random.
