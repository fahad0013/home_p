Security+ Acronyms and Ports Cheat Sheet (701 and 801)

By Prostutii Editorial • Last Verified: 2026-11-19
⚡Direct Answer / Summary
This Security+ cheat sheet gives you the common ports and protocols with their secure alternatives, plus nearly 100 exam acronyms grouped by domain, a table of commonly confused pairs, and memory techniques. Use it alongside either SY0-701 or SY0-801 study, and check the official objectives for your exam version.

Last verified: November 18, 2026, against CompTIA’s Security+ V7 and Security+ V8 pages. Print-friendly: use your browser’s Print or Save as PDF option to keep a copy.

Security+ rewards people who can recognize a term quickly and explain what it means in a scenario. This sheet puts the vocabulary on one page: the ports that tell you whether a service is secure, the acronyms grouped by the exam’s five domains, and the pairs of terms candidates mix up most often. Bookmark it, and test yourself by covering one column at a time.

Key takeaways
✦ Learn ports in pairs: an insecure service and its secure replacement (for example 80 and 443, or 23 and 22).
✦ Group acronyms by what they do, not alphabetically. Context is what makes them stick.
✦ Many exam traps are pairs of look-alike terms. The confused-pairs table is your fastest review.
✦ Some terms differ between SY0-701 and SY0-801, so confirm the official acronym list for your version.
✦ Understanding why a protocol is insecure matters more than reciting its number.

Pitfall: memorizing numbers without the reason.
A question rarely asks “what is the port for Telnet?” in isolation. It shows a scan with port 23 open and asks what to do. Know that Telnet sends credentials in clear text and that SSH on port 22 replaces it.

Security+ acronyms by domain

The terms below are grouped by the domain where they most often appear, and many show up in more than one. The meanings are written in our own words as a study aid. They are not CompTIA’s official list, so use the acronym list in the official objectives for your exam version to check anything you cannot find here.

Commonly confused pairs

Many Security+ distractors are real terms that answer a different question. For each pair, learn the single detail that separates the two.

PairThe difference
Authentication vs authorizationAuthentication proves who you are. Authorization decides what you may do.
Encryption vs hashingEncryption is reversible with a key and protects confidentiality. Hashing is one-way and checks integrity.
Symmetric vs asymmetricSymmetric uses one shared key and is fast for bulk data. Asymmetric uses a key pair and suits key exchange and signatures.
Virus vs wormA virus needs a host file or user action. A worm spreads on its own across networks.
Threat vs vulnerability vs riskA threat can cause harm, a vulnerability is a weakness, and risk is the likelihood and impact of the two meeting.
IDS vs IPSAn IDS detects and alerts. An IPS detects and blocks.
RTO vs RPORTO is how long recovery may take. RPO is how much data you can afford to lose, measured in time.
SLE vs ALESLE is the loss from one event. ALE is the expected loss per year (ALE = SLE × ARO).
CRL vs OCSPA CRL is a published list you download. OCSP checks one certificate’s status on request.
Phishing, vishing, smishingSame deception, different channels: email, voice call and SMS.
DoS vs DDoSA DoS attack comes from one source. A DDoS attack comes from many.
Detective vs deterrent controlsDetective controls find activity as or after it happens. Deterrent controls discourage it beforehand.
SAML vs OAuthSAML passes authentication assertions, often for single sign-on. OAuth lets an app act on a user’s behalf with delegated access.
RADIUS vs TACACS+RADIUS uses UDP and is common for network access such as Wi-Fi and VPN. TACACS+ uses TCP, separates authentication, authorization and accounting, and is common for device administration.
Hot, warm and cold sitesA hot site is ready to run now, a warm site is partly equipped, and a cold site offers space and power only.

Memory techniques that work

  1. Learn ports as secure pairs. 80 becomes 443, 23 becomes 22, 110 becomes 995, 143 becomes 993 and 389 becomes 636. Say each pair out loud as “insecure, then secure.”
  2. Tie each port to one sentence. “22 is how I log in safely to a server.” A sentence is easier to recall than a bare number.
  3. Use formulas as anchors. ALE = SLE × ARO. Think “single loss times how often per year.”
  4. Separate RTO and RPO. RTO is time to restore. RPO is the point in time you restore to, which tells you how much data you may lose.
  5. Cover and recall. Hide the right-hand column, say the meaning out loud, then check. Repeat missed items the next day.
  6. Test in context. After each study session, answer a few scenario questions from our General Security Concepts and Threats and vulnerabilities practice sets.
